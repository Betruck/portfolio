Energy Systems & Thermofluids

10 kW Crossflow Turbine

Led runner sizing and overall design of a 10 kW Michell-Banki crossflow turbine for off-grid rural electrification, developed with a 3-member team and selected over Pelton/Kaplan designs for its self-cleaning, low-head performance.

Hydraulic DesignStructural AnalysisCAD
Design Proposal

My role: Led runner sizing and the overall turbine design within a 3-member team, covering the hydraulic and geometric calculations that defined the rest of the system.

Problem: Rural communities near small rivers often have real hydraulic potential but rely on diesel generators or biomass, since most turbine technologies are too costly or unsuited to low-head, silt-heavy conditions.

Why crossflow: Compared against Pelton (needs >50 m head — impractical for rural sites) and Kaplan (high efficiency but complex, precision-dependent, and cavitation-prone), the crossflow topology was selected for its low-to-medium head range, basic local maintainability, and low cost.

  • Hydraulic sizing — power availability calculated from P = ρ·g·Q·H, with runner outer/inner diameter derived from the target rotational speed and standard diameter ratio.
  • Blade geometry — jet attack angle α = 16°, giving a blade angle β₁ ≈ 30°, per standard crossflow design relations.
  • Runner fabrication — 20 blades formed by longitudinally cutting schedule-40 stainless steel pipe (4-inch diameter) into quarter-pipe segments, keeping fabrication local and low-cost.
  • Power transmission — belt-and-pulley drive matching the turbine's low rotational speed to a 10 kW alternator running at 1500 RPM.

Status: Design and analysis complete as a Machine Design course proposal; physical fabrication is the next planned phase.

Biogas Generator Conversion

Converted a gasoline engine to run on biogas for 5 kW power generation, redesigning the fuel-air mixing system, ignition timing, and compression ratio for stable combustion.

Combustion SystemsMechanical Conversion
In progress

Conversion approach: Adapted a standard gasoline engine to run on biogas by replacing the carburetor with a gas-mixing (venturi-type) valve suited to biogas's lower calorific value and slower flame speed.

  • Ignition timing — advanced to compensate for methane's slower combustion rate relative to gasoline.
  • Compression ratio — evaluated against the engine's baseline setting to balance knock resistance with achievable power output.
  • Fuel delivery — sized the mixer and intake path for biogas's lower energy density, accounting for the power derating typical of gasoline-to-biogas conversions.
  • Target output — 5 kW continuous power generation from the converted unit.

Carbonized Coffee Husk Briquettes

Developing carbonized briquettes from coffee husk waste as a renewable, eco-friendly alternative to wood charcoal — reducing deforestation while turning an abundant coffee-processing byproduct into usable cooking fuel.

Waste-to-EnergyCombustion KineticsOriginPro
In progress

Problem: Kenya's reliance on wood charcoal for household cooking drives deforestation and emissions, while coffee husks — a large-volume processing byproduct — are largely discarded rather than reused.

Approach: Coffee husks are carbonized and formed into briquettes with a suitable binder using a fabricated briquetting machine, then evaluated against key fuel-performance properties:

  • Calorific value
  • Moisture and ash content
  • Ignition time and burning rate
  • Combustion duration, benchmarked against traditional wood charcoal

Goal: A durable, energy-saving briquette that cuts agricultural waste and reduces reliance on charcoal from wood, supporting renewable energy and sustainable use of agricultural residues.

Sustainable Design

Re-Block Extruder

A thermochemical extrusion system converting recycled LDPE plastic and river sand into interlocking paving tiles — a lighter, more durable, and more cost-effective alternative to concrete "cabro" blocks.

CADRecyclingProduct Design
Proposal Stage

Concept: Re-Block addresses two problems at once — Kenya's growing plastic waste crisis and the structural shortcomings of traditional concrete masonry — by converting recycled LDPE and sand into interlocking tiles via a proprietary extrusion process operating at 180–220°C.

  • Structural performance — 35 MPa compressive strength, meeting industrial load-bearing standards.
  • Durability — near-zero water absorption, preventing the cracking, moss growth, and salt dampness common in concrete.
  • Logistics — 25% lighter than concrete equivalents, enabling higher transport payloads and faster, mortar-less installation.
  • Geometry — a specialized "S-Curve" interlocking profile for structural stability under heavy vehicle traffic.

Mechanical Design & Simulation

7.74:1 Speed Reducer Gearbox

Designed a 7.74:1 compound reverted helical gearbox for a 78.5 kW drive, validated through AGMA gear-stress and shaft-fatigue analysis in SolidWorks.

Gear Train DesignFEAShaft Design

Brief: 78.5 kW drive, reducing 1200 rpm input to 155 rpm output — a 7.7419:1 ratio — at a minimum Factor of Safety of 2.0, 20,000-hour service life, and 95% reliability.

  • Kinematic synthesis — split the ratio across two gear stages in a compound reverted layout, solving tooth counts (20 / 75 / 31 / 64) to satisfy the coaxial center-distance constraint (295 mm).
  • AGMA gear stress analysis — verified bending and contact stress on every mesh, factoring in dynamic, load-distribution, and overload effects.
  • Shaft design & fatigue analysis — sized the input, intermediate, and output shafts (AISI 1050 CD) against combined bending/torsional fatigue, achieving FOS 2.04–2.45 across all three.
  • Bearing selection — thrust roller bearings sized for a 20,000-hour L10 life.
  • CAD verification — modeled the full assembly in SolidWorks, including a cast-iron housing sized for manufacturability.

Prosthetic Leg Design

Designed a lower-limb prosthetic leg, modeling the socket, pylon, and joint assembly in CAD and evaluating structural strength under simulated gait loading to balance durability with weight.

Biomechanical DesignFEACAD

Lower-limb prostheses are built around three core elements — a custom socket, a structural pylon, and articulating joint components — with alignment between them being the single biggest factor in comfort, stability, and gait efficiency.

  • Socket design — modeled to closely match residual-limb geometry, since fit quality directly affects load distribution and wearer comfort.
  • Alignment — socket, pylon, and foot positioned to support a natural gait through heel strike, stance, and toe-off, balancing stability against mobility.
  • Structural validation — modeled under simulated gait loading to verify the assembly can withstand cyclic stance-phase forces without failure, in line with standard lower-limb prosthetic testing principles.

Flow Simulation in a Designed Tank

Modeled a process tank in SolidWorks and ran Flow Simulation to evaluate internal flow patterns, velocity distribution, and pressure drop.

CFDSolidWorks Flow SimulationFluid Dynamics

Internal flow behavior was evaluated using SolidWorks Flow Simulation's finite-volume CFD solver, which meshes the actual fluid volume inside the tank rather than the solid geometry.

  • Turbulence modeling — resolved via SolidWorks' built-in k-ε model, standard for internal flow at typical process-tank Reynolds numbers.
  • Boundary conditions — inlet flow rate/velocity and outlet pressure defined to represent real operating conditions.
  • Outputs analyzed — velocity distribution, pressure drop, and flow trajectory, used to assess dead zones and mixing behavior.
  • Design feedback loop — results informed inlet/outlet placement and internal geometry to improve flow uniformity.

Heat Sink Simulation

Simulated the thermal performance of a heat sink design, analyzing fin geometry and airflow to evaluate heat dissipation and surface temperature distribution under a defined thermal load.

Thermal AnalysisHeat TransferCFD/FEA

Thermal performance was evaluated by simulating conduction through the heat sink body and convective heat loss from the fin surfaces, a standard approach for electronics-cooling design in SolidWorks.

  • Material selection — trade-off between aluminum (lighter, lower cost) and copper (higher thermal conductivity, heavier) evaluated for the application.
  • Fin geometry — height, thickness, and spacing sized to maximize surface area while keeping airflow resistance manageable.
  • Convection mode — analyzed under natural convection, where fin spacing must be wider than in forced-air designs to avoid restricting buoyancy-driven airflow.
  • Output — temperature distribution across the base and fins, used to identify hotspots and verify the design meets the target thermal load.
